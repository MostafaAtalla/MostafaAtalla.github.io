What you see. The hand pulls the block through a spring at constant speed. Because static friction exceeds kinetic friction, force builds until the block breaks free, overshoots and sticks again. The force trace becomes a sawtooth: stick-slip.

Switch on the vibration. A squeeze film forms under the contact and carries part of the load. Effective friction drops and the gap between static and kinetic friction narrows, so the sawtooth collapses into a smooth glide at lower force.

Try it. Drag the handle yourself, raise μs to exaggerate the jumps, or lower the amplitude to find where gliding begins.

Illustrative model with Coulomb friction and assumed friction reductions; not fitted to experimental data.
